# E2 — Validation against measured human abundance

**Why it is needed.** B1 reports ρ = 0.839 on held-out SH3 domains and B3 calls 410 atlas variants
confidently destabilising. Both are predictions, evaluated against the same MegaScale assay the model
was trained on. E2 asks whether they hold against a **different assay, in a different laboratory, on
human domains, for variants that actually occur in tumours** — with no wet lab of our own.

**What it does not do.** It trains nothing and changes no frozen number. It is evaluation-only: if
E2 had failed, A0–D1 would still stand as reported, and the failure would be the finding.

## Inputs
| File | From | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `Extended_data_Table_2…domainranks.txt` | Domainome v5 | `normalized_fitness` — measured abundance |
| `atlas_predictions.parquet` | B3 (frozen) | `pred_ddG_fold`, `fold_lo` |
| `SH3_cancer_master.tsv` | A0 (frozen) | protein positions for the join |
| `domains_from_tsuboyama_rocklin_overlaps.txt` | Domainome archive | which domains were measured twice |
| `K50_dG_Dataset1_Dataset2_in_aPCA.csv` | Domainome archive | MegaScale ΔΔG, for the ceiling |

## Method
1. Deduplicate the Domainome table (the two defects X0 documents, repeated here so E2 stands alone).
2. Join to the frozen predictions on `uniprot / pos_protein / wt_aa / mut_aa`, asserting the atlas
   row count is unchanged.
3. Spearman ρ, pooled and per domain (n ≥ 20).
4. Mann–Whitney: do the `fold_lo ≥ 1` calls separate from the rest?
5. **The noise ceiling.** Seven SH3 domains were measured by *both* MegaScale and Domainome. Their
   agreement bounds what any predictor could reach. Computed on all shared variants, and again on
   the subset we predict so the two are directly comparable.

## The two traps
**Signs.** `pred_ddG_fold` is positive for destabilising; both `normalized_fitness` and Rocklin ΔΔG
are negative. So ours correlates *negatively* with fitness and the two assays correlate *positively*
with each other. Everything is reported as |ρ|. Getting this backwards silently inverts the result.

**Positions.** Rocklin positions are domain-local (1–70); Domainome's are absolute (215–276). The
conversion is `rocklin_pos = domainome_position − domain_start + 1 + offset`. Joining on absolute
positions gives **zero** matches — silently, because a zero-row inner join looks like "no overlap".
Verified empirically: 1,102 BTK matches at the recorded offset against ≤114 at any other shift.

## Results
**880 of 6,884 atlas cancer variants have a direct Domainome measurement**, across 41 domains and
32 genes. Pooled |ρ| = **0.600** (p = 4 × 10⁻⁸⁷); per-domain median 0.573, correct direction in
**21 / 21**. The `fold_lo ≥ 1` calls separate cleanly: median fitness −0.748 against −0.106,
p = 2.3 × 10⁻²².

**The ceiling is 0.709.** Two independent experiments measuring the same variants agree only that
well, so 0.600 is near the achievable limit, not a moderate correlation. On matched variants the head
reaches **90–113 %** of the ceiling.

This also corrected a misreading: HCK looked like the worst domain (0.339), but on matched variants
its ceiling is 0.593 against our 0.604 — the weakness was in *which* variants were compared.

## Known defects
NPHP1 (1S1N) and RASA1 (2M51) produce no matched variants: both PDB entries are present in the aPCA
file, but the recorded offsets do not reconcile their numbering. Their ceilings are unknown. The
matched-subset ratios rest on 19–39 variants and are indicative, not precise.
