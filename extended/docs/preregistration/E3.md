# E3 — The rules, written down before we look

*(DRAFT — not signed off, not committed yet)*

## What this file is for

E3 asks: **how does our model compare with the best current stability predictors?**

The competitors are SaProtΔG, ESM3ΔG, ThermoMPNN and ProteinDPO. Two of them (SaProtΔG, ESM3ΔG)
come in several versions and need structure files, so there are choices to make. We make them
**now**, run several experiments, and **compare them at the end**. This file is committed to git
first; only then is the notebook written.

Once committed, this file is never edited. Changes go in a dated note, and the original plan's
results are still reported.

**So far:** no competitor has been run on our test domains. The leakage audit (below) is done, but it
checks training data, not results.

---

## Part 1 — Rules shared by every experiment

### The test
- **The 15 held-out SH3 domains from B1** — none of them were used to train our model.
- **Exactly the same variants B1 used**, domain by domain. Every competitor is scored on the same
  variants as our model; a variant a competitor cannot score is dropped for **everyone**, and the
  count dropped is reported.
- **Our model's numbers come from B1, frozen.** Nothing about our model is re-run or changed.

### The measure
- **Per domain:** Spearman correlation between prediction and measured stability (as in B1).
- **Overall:** the median across the 15 domains.
- **Signs checked first:** every model is aligned so that "positive = destabilising" before
  comparing (E2's trap).

### The leakage check — already done
SaProtΔG and ESM3ΔG were trained on data that includes SH3 domains. We searched both published
training lists by sequence: **0 of 15** held-out domains appear in either. This proves absence from
the *published* lists, not from whatever the final model files were trained on. The report says
exactly that, no more.

### What counts as a difference
For each competitor, we compare its 15 per-domain correlations with ours, domain against domain
(paired Wilcoxon test).

- **Our model better / competitor better:** p < 0.0125, and the direction.
- **No clear difference:** otherwise.

*(0.0125 = 0.05 split across four competitors.)*

We always report *how big* the gap is (median difference), not only whether it exists. With only 15
domains, small gaps will usually show as "no clear difference" — that is a limit of the test, and
the report says so.

## Part 2 — The experiments

ThermoMPNN and ProteinDPO predictions come ready-made from the MGnify file, so they are the same in
every experiment. The experiments change how SaProtΔG and ESM3ΔG are run — **one thing at a time**
from Experiment 1.

| Exp | Model version | Structures | Seeds | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — MAIN** | **Base** | **Measured constructs** ¹ | **Average of all 3** | The main comparison |
| 2 | **Augmented** | Measured constructs | Average of 3 | What does extra training data buy? |
| 3 | Base | **AlphaFold, trimmed to domain** ² | Average of 3 | Does the structure source matter? |
| 4 | Base | Measured constructs | **Each seed alone** | How much do seeds vary? |

¹ The structure of the exact protein piece MegaScale measured. Its numbering matches MegaScale
positions directly.

² The full-length AlphaFold model (as fetched by A2), cut to the domain boundaries, with positions
mapped by the recorded offsets.

### Why these choices for the main experiment
- **Base:** trained on the same kind of data as our model, so the comparison is about the *method*,
  not about who had more data. *(Alternative reason, if chosen instead: augmented is the version the
  authors present as their best. Decide which, and cross out the other: ______)*
- **Measured constructs:** they match what was actually measured, and avoid position-mapping errors.
- **Average of 3 seeds:** choosing the best seed after seeing results is the same trap as choosing the
  best threshold.

### Fallbacks, decided now
- **Measured-construct structures unavailable** for a domain → that domain uses AlphaFold in
  Experiment 1, and the report lists which.
- **ESM3ΔG cannot be run** (licence or hardware) → it is reported as "not run", and the other three
  competitors stand. No substitute is chosen later.

### How the table will be read — decided now
1. **The verdict is Experiment 1's.** Always.
2. **Robust:** the other experiments agree → the result does not depend on version, structures or
   seed. We say so.
3. **Depends on a choice:** one flips → we name which (for example, "competitor X is better with
   augmented training, level with base"). Reported as a finding.
4. **No experiment is promoted to main after seeing results.**
5. **If the competitors win**, that is reported plainly. The project's contribution is the separation
   of mechanisms and the positional-coverage limit, not a leaderboard position — and a better
   stability predictor would make the 410 calls better, not worse.

## Part 4 — Known weaknesses, admitted in advance

- **Unequal inputs.** SaProtΔG and ESM3ΔG receive a 3D structure; our model never did. If they win,
  part of the reason may be that extra information.
- **Some competitors saw these proteins' data.** 13 of the 15 domains appear in the MGnify
  *measurement* file, even though not in the published training lists. We cannot rule out that the
  final model files saw them.
- **15 domains is few.** The test can detect large gaps, not small ones.

## Part 5 — Review before sign-off (27 Sep 2026)

Three things were checked against the actual data before this file is signed.

### 5.1 ⚠ ThermoMPNN and ProteinDPO are not available for our test domains

Part 2 says their predictions *"come ready-made from the MGnify file, so they are the same in every
experiment"*. They do not.

`fig3__megascale_point_mutants_predictions.csv` covers **26 wild-type proteins**:

```
1GYZ 1PSE 1QKH 1QP2 1TUC 1W4H 2BTH 2K1B 2K28 2K5H 2KVS 2KWH 2KXD 2L33
2LQK 2WXC 3DKM 4C26 6EWS 6EWT 6EWU  +  HEEH HHH r11 r12 r18 (designed proteins)
```

Our 15 held-out domains map to `1S1N 1H92 6IPY 6NMW 4HCK 1QLY 1PWT 2CDT 2LJ3 6SCW 2KGT 3I35 1ZLM
2O2W`. **The overlap is empty.** That file is the authors' own 26-protein benchmark subset, not a
prediction for all of MegaScale.

**Decision required.** Either:

- **(a) Run ThermoMPNN ourselves.** It is open source and light — no licence gate, far cheaper than
  ESM3. ProteinDPO is harder to obtain; it would likely be dropped.
- **(b) Drop both**, leaving SaProtΔG and ESM3ΔG. The comparison is then against two predictors, not
  four.

This also changes the significance threshold. Part 1 sets p < 0.0125 as 0.05 split across four
competitors. **With two competitors it becomes 0.025; with three, 0.0167.** Fix the denominator by
the number of competitors *attempted*, decided now — not by how many happen to produce a number.

### 5.2 The base-versus-augmented choice is still blank

Part 2 leaves it open: *"Decide which, and cross out the other: ______"*. A pre-registration with an
unresolved choice in it is not pre-registered. This must be filled before sign-off.

**Recommendation: base.** The stated reason is the better one — it makes the comparison about the
method rather than about who had more training data, and "augmented" exists precisely because the
authors added data. Keeping augmented as Experiment 2 already answers "what does the extra data
buy?", which is the more interesting question anyway.

### 5.3 Confirm the measured-construct structures exist before committing to them

Experiment 1 specifies the structure of the exact construct MegaScale measured, on the grounds that
its numbering matches MegaScale positions directly. That is the right choice **if the files are
there**. The count of domains for which they are available has not been checked.

**Do this before sign-off**, because the fallback ("that domain uses AlphaFold in Experiment 1") is
sound for one or two domains and hollow if it fires for twelve — at which point Experiment 3 is
really the main experiment under another name.

### 5.4 What this review does not change

The leakage audit stands: 0 of 15 held-out domains in either published training split, with the
scope caveat already stated correctly. The reading rules, the Wilcoxon design, the sign alignment
and the honest note that 15 domains can only detect large gaps all stand as written.
